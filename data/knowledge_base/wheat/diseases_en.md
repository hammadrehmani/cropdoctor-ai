# Wheat Disease Management Guide (English)

## Leaf Rust (Brown Rust) — Puccinia triticina
- **Crop**: Wheat
- **Symptoms**: Small, round to oval orange-brown powdery pustules (uredinia) scattered on the upper surface of wheat leaves. Severe infection causes early leaf senescence and poor grain filling.
- **Favourable Conditions**: Temperatures between 15°C and 22°C with high relative humidity (>80%) or extended morning dew.
- **Prevention & Cultural Control**: Sow certified rust-resistant varieties recommended by PARC/NARC (such as Faisalabad-2008, Akbar-2019, Dilkash-2020). Avoid delayed planting after November.
- **Field Monitoring**: Inspect lower and middle leaves weekly during booting and heading stages.
- **Management**: Apply registered systemic triazole fungicides according to the official product label at the earliest appearance of rust pustules.
- **When to Contact an Expert**: If pustules cover more than 15% of the upper flag leaf canopy or spread rapidly despite early intervention, contact local extension officers.

## Yellow Rust (Stripe Rust) — Puccinia striiformis
- **Crop**: Wheat
- **Symptoms**: Distinct bright yellow to orange pustules arranged in linear stripes parallel to the leaf veins. In severe attacks, chlorotic streaks cover the entire leaf blade.
- **Favourable Conditions**: Cool temperatures (7°C to 15°C) with persistent moisture, fog, and overcast conditions in northern and central regions.
- **Prevention & Cultural Control**: Plant stripe rust-tolerant cultivars. Destroy volunteer wheat plants and crop debris around field borders.
- **Field Monitoring**: Scout fields starting from tillering stage in December through February, particularly in cooler hollows and shaded field margins.
- **Management**: Use label-approved protective fungicides on recommendation from local agriculture departments.
- **When to Contact an Expert**: If stripe rust is observed before heading stage across multiple field zones, immediately alert the regional agricultural extension office.

## Powdery Mildew — Blumeria graminis f. sp. tritici
- **Crop**: Wheat
- **Symptoms**: White to light-grey powdery fungal patches on the upper surface of leaves, stems, and leaf sheaths. With age, the patches turn dull grey-brown with tiny black fruiting bodies.
- **Favourable Conditions**: Dense crop canopies, high humidity (85–100%), and moderate temperatures (15–20°C) with low sunlight.
- **Prevention & Cultural Control**: Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilization; maintain balanced N-P-K nutrition and optimal seed rate to prevent dense foliage.
- **Management**: Implement preventive canopy aeration; consult certified agronomists for registered foliar treatments if upper leaves become colonized.
