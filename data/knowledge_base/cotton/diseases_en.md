# Cotton Disease Management Guide (English)

## Cotton Leaf Curl Disease (CLCuD)
- **Crop**: Cotton
- **Symptoms**: Upward or downward curling of leaves, thickened dark-green veins on the underside, leaf-like outgrowths (enations), and severe stunting with reduced boll production.
- **Vector & Cause**: Transmitted by the whitefly (*Bemisia tabaci*) carrying cotton leaf curl begomovirus.
- **Favourable Conditions**: High whitefly population density, warm temperatures (28°C to 38°C), and early vegetative growth stages.
- **Prevention & Integrated Management**: Sow CLCuD-tolerant transgenic and conventional varieties recommended by provincial agriculture departments. Control whitefly vectors using recommended IPM practices. Rogue out and bury infected early volunteer plants.
- **When to Contact an Expert**: If whitefly counts exceed economic threshold levels (5 adults/nymphs per leaf) or leaf curl appears in early squaring.

## Bacterial Blight (Angular Leaf Spot / Black Arm) — Xanthomonas citri
- **Crop**: Cotton
- **Symptoms**: Angular water-soaked lesions bounded by leaf veins, turning dark brown to black. On stems, black elongate lesions ("black arm") cause lodging and branch loss.
- **Favourable Conditions**: Warm temperatures (25–35°C), high humidity, and rainstorms with driving winds.
- **Prevention & Management**: Use certified acid-delinted seed. Practice crop rotation. Avoid sprinkler or overhead irrigation.

## Alternaria Leaf Spot — Alternaria macrospora
- **Crop**: Cotton
- **Symptoms**: Small circular brown spots with distinct concentric rings, surrounded by yellow halos, causing premature defoliation.
- **Favourable Conditions**: Extended leaf wetness, humidity over 85%, and mature boll-load stage.
- **Prevention & Management**: Maintain adequate soil potassium fertility. Ensure good field drainage and canopy ventilation.
